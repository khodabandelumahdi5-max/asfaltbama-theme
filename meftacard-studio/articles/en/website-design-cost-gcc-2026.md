# How Much Does a Website Cost in the GCC in 2026? A Buyer's Breakdown

Ask five agencies in Dubai or Riyadh for a website quote and you will get five numbers that differ by a factor of ten. That is not because four of them are wrong. It is because "a website" can mean a three-page brochure, a bilingual catalog with an RFQ funnel, or a corporate platform with supplier portals and an uptime SLA.

This guide breaks down website design cost in Dubai, Riyadh and the wider Gulf the way a procurement team would: what the published ranges are, what actually moves the number, the costs that are usually left out of the first quote, and how to compare proposals fairly.

## The published ranges, and why they disagree

Agency pricing guides published in 2026 give a wide spread:

| Market | Typical range quoted | What it usually covers |
|---|---|---|
| Dubai / UAE | AED 8,000 – 25,000 for a 5–10 page business site ([Element8](https://www.element8.ae/blogs/website-design-cost-dubai)) | Template or semi-custom design, CMS, basic SEO |
| Dubai / UAE | From AED 1,500 basic, AED 50,000+ for custom or e-commerce ([Saad Ashraf](https://saadashraf.net/blog/?p=5695)) | From DIY-level builds to custom platforms |
| Saudi Arabia | SAR 60,000 – 120,000 for a business website ([Apptunix](https://www.apptunix.com/blog/?p=60268)) | Custom design and development, integrations |

Treat these as marketing estimates, not benchmarks. Every source is an agency that sells websites, and their definitions of "business website" do not match. The useful takeaway is the shape of the market: a low end dominated by templates and freelancers, a middle band of semi-custom agency work, and an enterprise tier where the website is one part of a larger system.

## The seven things that actually set the price

### 1. Number of templates, not number of pages
A 40-page site built from four page templates (home, service, article, contact) can cost less than a 12-page site where every page is designed from scratch. Ask how many unique layouts are in scope.

### 2. Languages and direction
A properly bilingual Arabic/English site is not a translation plugin. Arabic needs right-to-left layouts, Arabic typography, separate URLs and hreflang so Google serves the right version. Agency guides put Arabic localisation at roughly AED 3,000–8,000 on top of the base build. If a quote includes "Arabic" for free, check whether it is machine translation on the same URL. That approach hurts rankings in both languages (see our [Arabic SEO guide](https://meftacard.com/arabic-seo-hreflang-guide/)).

### 3. Content
Copywriting, product data entry, photography and datasheets are frequently excluded. For industrial and export companies, content preparation can take longer than design.

### 4. Integrations
CRM, ERP, WhatsApp Business, payment gateways (Mada and STC Pay in Saudi Arabia, local wallets elsewhere) and booking systems each add development and testing time.

### 5. Performance and SEO foundations
Fast mobile loading, schema markup, clean URL structure and Search Console setup cost little when built in from day one and a lot when retrofitted. A site that is invisible in search is a brochure nobody reads.

### 6. Security, hosting and compliance
Managed hosting, backups, SSL, a web application firewall and, for regulated sectors, data-residency requirements all carry recurring costs.

### 7. Who owns what afterwards
Some low quotes are low because the agency keeps the code, the domain or the hosting account. Ownership should be written into the contract.

## The costs that rarely appear in the first quote

- **Domain and hosting renewals.** A .com typically runs AED 50–100 a year; .ae and .sa domains cost more.
- **Licences.** Premium themes, plugins, fonts and stock images.
- **Maintenance.** Updates, security patches and small edits. Budget for it monthly rather than discovering it at the first incident.
- **Content updates and SEO.** Rankings come from continuous publishing, not from launch day.
- **Change requests.** Anything outside the written scope.

## How to compare three quotes fairly

1. **Send the same written brief to everyone:** pages or templates, languages, integrations, content responsibility, deadline.
2. **Ask for a fixed scope and fixed price**, with change requests priced in writing before work starts.
3. **Ask for the performance target.** A serious agency will commit to a mobile loading target and show it on past work.
4. **Ask who owns the code, domain and accounts.** The answer should be "you, from day one".
5. **Ask what happens after launch:** support period, response time and the monthly cost of keeping the site healthy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a $300 website a real option?**
For a personal page, perhaps. For a business that needs to rank and convert, the hidden costs (rebuilds, lost leads, poor performance) usually exceed the savings within a year.

**How long does a business website take?**
A focused local site can launch in about two weeks; a multilingual catalog in about a month; an enterprise platform in roughly three to four months, depending on content and approvals.

**Should we build in Arabic, English or both?**
If your buyers search in both languages, both. Build them as separate URLs with hreflang from the start; adding Arabic later is more expensive than planning for it.
