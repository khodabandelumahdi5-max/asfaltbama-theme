# The B2B Export Website: Turning International Search into RFQs

For a manufacturer or trader, the website has one commercial job: to produce **qualified requests for quotation** from buyers you have never met. Not likes, not "brand awareness". RFQs with a product, a quantity and a delivery destination.

This guide shows how to build a B2B export website that does that job. Most export websites are built as company brochures and then wonder why the contact form stays quiet. This guide explains how to structure an export site around the way international buyers actually search and buy.

## How international B2B buyers search

A procurement officer in Riyadh, Erbil or Muscat looking for a supplier rarely searches for your company name. They search for:

- **The product plus a specification:** "galvanized steel pipe ASTM A53", "HDPE pipe PN16".
- **The product plus a location:** "cable manufacturer UAE", "building materials supplier Iraq".
- **The product plus a commercial term:** "bitumen 60/70 price", "wholesale".

Each of these lands on a **product or category page**, not your home page. That is where the site wins or loses the RFQ.

## The anatomy of a product page that generates RFQs

1. **A precise title** that includes the product name and key specification.
2. **A specification table** covering grades, sizes, standards, packaging and minimum order quantity. Buyers compare tables, not paragraphs.
3. **Certifications and test reports** relevant to that product.
4. **A downloadable datasheet** with a clear file name, also summarised in HTML so search engines can index it.
5. **Lead time, Incoterms and shipping regions** you serve.
6. **A short RFQ form on the page itself**, pre-filled with the product name.
7. **Instant contact options**: WhatsApp or Telegram for buyers who prefer a quick conversation.

## Design the RFQ form for conversion

Long forms kill RFQs; vague forms waste your sales team's time. The balance:

| Ask for | Skip (ask later) |
|---|---|
| Product (pre-filled), quantity, destination country | Company registration number |
| Name, company, email or WhatsApp | Full postal address |
| Optional: specification notes or file upload | Budget |

Route every RFQ to a named person with a response-time target, and log it in a CRM so nothing is lost.

## International SEO essentials for exporters

- **Category architecture.** Group products the way buyers think (by application or standard), not by your internal SKU codes.
- **One page per product family**, with variants handled in tables rather than dozens of near-duplicate pages.
- **Schema markup** (Product, Organization, BreadcrumbList) so search engines understand the catalog.
- **Fast, mobile-friendly pages.** Many B2B buyers in the region research on their phones.
- **Content that answers buyer questions:** comparisons of standards, selection guides, packaging and shipping explainers. These pages attract searches early in the buying process and link to product pages.

## Trust signals that matter to foreign buyers

- Physical address, trade licence or company registration details.
- Export history, markets served and, where permitted, client references.
- Factory or warehouse photos. Real ones, not stock images.
- Quality certifications with scope and validity.
- Clear payment and documentation terms.

## Measure what matters

Set up analytics around the RFQ, not around traffic:

- RFQs per month, by product and country.
- Product pages that receive traffic but no RFQs, which signals weak content or a weak form.
- Response time to RFQs.
- RFQ-to-order conversion rate, tracked in your CRM.
